# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this repository is

A personal reference collection of alternative-medicine books (homeopathy, Ayurveda, Unani, plus a `general` bucket for cross-disciplinary works), plus a Python pipeline that turns the raw source files into a clean, searchable local database. There is no application code to build/lint/test — the only "code" is the two scripts in `tools/` (`process_book.py`, `build_library_db.py`); `catalogue_seed.json` there is hand-maintained metadata, not code.

**Copyright note:** almost every book here is an older work with a plausible (if unverified) public-domain argument. Eleven are not: four in `raw/general/` -- `Marc S. Micozzi - Fundamentals of Complementary and Alternative Medicine - 5th Edition.epub` (2014 Elsevier textbook), `Edzard Ernst et al. - Oxford Handbook of Complementary Medicine.epub` (Oxford University Press handbook, likely from an Archive.org Controlled Digital Lending scan — a lending-restriction bypass, not just an ambiguous-vintage work), `Michael Heinrich et al. - Fundamentals of Pharmacognosy and Phytotherapy - 4th Edition.epub` (Elsevier VitalSource retail textbook with a leftover Adobe ADEPT DRM tag proving it was stripped from a purchased copy), and `Joanne Barnes et al. - Herbal Medicines - 3rd Edition.epub` (2007 Pharmaceutical Press reference text, downloaded from a third-party site rather than any ambiguous-vintage archive) -- three in `raw/unani/`: `DGHS - The Unani Pharmacopia of Bangladesh - Part 1 Volume 4 (2020).pdf` (a 2020 Government of Bangladesh publication with its own explicit ownership/copyright line in the front matter; unlike US federal works, Bangladeshi government publications are not automatically public domain), `AYUSH - National Formulary of Unani Medicine - Part 2 Volume 1 (2007).pdf` (a 2007 Government of India/AYUSH publication, ISBN 81-87748-02-8 -- no explicit rights-reserved notice found, but still a modern government-owned work under Indian copyright law), and `Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyyah - Healing with the Medicine of the Prophet - Trans. Abd El-Qader ibn Abd El-Azeez - 2003.epub` (a 2003 English translation of Ibn Qayyim's "Al-Tibb al-Nabawi" / Prophetic Medicine -- a hadith-based Islamic medicine tradition distinct from the Greco-Arabic Unani medicine of Ibn Sina's Canon, filed under `unani` as the closest existing bucket; carries an explicit "EXCLUSIVE RIGHTS BY DAR AL-GHADD AL-GADEED" notice from its Egyptian publisher, Al-Mansoura, 2003 -- one of the clearest rights-reserved statements in this collection; note a different, more widely-known translation of the same title, Jalal Abualrub/Darussalam 2003, exists but is CDL-restricted and was not used -- the two are not the same edition) -- and four in `raw/ayurveda/`: `Oushadhi - Therapeutic Index - 5th Impression (2019).pdf` (a 2019 publication from a Government of Kerala state undertaking, carrying the collection's most explicit copyright statement: "Copy right reserved. No part of this publication may be translated or transmitted...without permission"), `Ashtanga Hridaya Vagbhatta English Trans Srikantha Murthy K.R. Vol 1 Chowkambha.epub` (a likely ~2004 Chowkhamba Krishnadas Academy translation -- retroactively flagged; this entry predates this collection's copyright-flagging practice and was originally added without a year or note, an oversight rather than a deliberate call), `Bhavamishra - Bhavaprakasha Vol 1 (with Nighantu) - Trans. K.R. Srikantha Murthy - 2001.epub` (a 2001 Krishnadas Academy translation -- same translator/publisher family and era as the Ashtanga Hridaya entry, added to fill the "Laghu Trayi" materia-medica/nighantu gap alongside the homeopathy repertories), and `Madhavakara - Madhava Nidana (Ayurvedic Diagnostics) Vol 1 - Trans. Kanjiv Lochan, ed. Brahmanand Tripathi.epub` (a modern Chaukhamba Surbharati Prakashan translation, Volume I of II only -- the fuller work runs 79 chapters and no complete English edition was found). All eleven were added (or, for the retroactively-flagged Ashtanga Hridaya, confirmed) at the user's explicit request after being flagged as copyright concerns — each time asked and confirmed separately, not as a standing blanket approval. Don't treat this as precedent for adding other modern copyrighted works without the same explicit, informed confirmation — and think carefully before this repo is ever pushed somewhere public while these files remain in `raw/`.

## Data flow

```
raw/<discipline>/<file>.epub|.pdf   (original, untouched, as-downloaded)
        |  tools/process_book.py
        v
processed/<discipline>/<book-id>/{book.epub, book.json}   (clean text, page-anchored)
processed/catalogue.json                                  (aggregated metadata index -- HAND-MAINTAINED, see below)
        |  tools/build_library_db.py
        v
db/library.db   (SQLite, FTS5 full-text search — git-ignored, regenerable build artifact)
```

`raw/` is source of truth and is never edited in place. Everything downstream from it is derived and can be regenerated at any time by rerunning `process_book.py`, with one exception: **`processed/catalogue.json` is not generated by either script** -- `process_book.py` only ever writes to `processed/<discipline>/<book-id>/`, never to `processed/catalogue.json` itself. It must be kept in sync with `tools/catalogue_seed.json` by hand whenever a catalogue entry is added or edited (see "Adding a new book" below); `build_library_db.py` will happily build a database from a stale `processed/catalogue.json` without warning. This was found out of sync once already (`ashtanga-hridaya-vagbhatta-vol-1`'s retroactive copyright note was added to `catalogue_seed.json` but `process_book.py` was never rerun for it, so its `book.json` was missing the note -- fixed 2026-09-25).

## Commands

```sh
pip3 install -r tools/requirements.txt      # pypdf, for PDF sources

python3 tools/process_book.py --all         # regenerate processed/ for every catalogued book
python3 tools/process_book.py <book-id>     # regenerate a single book (id = key in tools/catalogue_seed.json)

python3 tools/build_library_db.py           # rebuild db/library.db from processed/catalogue.json
```

Query example (full-text search over page text):

```sh
sqlite3 db/library.db "
  SELECT b.title, p.page_number, snippet(pages_fts, 0, '[', ']', '...', 12)
  FROM pages_fts
  JOIN pages p ON p.id = pages_fts.rowid
  JOIN books b ON b.id = p.book_id
  WHERE pages_fts MATCH 'aconite'
  ORDER BY rank LIMIT 5;
"
```

## Adding a new book

1. Place the original file under `raw/<discipline>/` using `Author - Title - Edition.ext`.
2. Add a matching entry to `tools/catalogue_seed.json` (`id`, `discipline`, `raw_file`, `title`, `author`, `year`, `language`, etc.). **Metadata must be filled in by hand** — the source EPUBs/PDFs carry no usable `dc:title`/`dc:creator` (Internet Archive exports only embed a random UUID and accessibility boilerplate), so `process_book.py` has nothing to read metadata from. If the source is a pure page-scan with no text layer at all, also set `"source_format": "facsimile-epub"` and `"ocr_lang"` (a `tesseract` language code) — see "Source file quirks" below.
3. Add the book to the collection table in `README.md`.
4. Rerun `process_book.py --all` (or the single id).
5. Add a matching entry to `processed/catalogue.json` by hand (title, author, language, discipline, page count from the generated `book.json`, plus `source_raw_file`/`processed_epub`/`processed_json` paths) — this file is not generated by `process_book.py`, so it's easy to forget. When editing an *existing* book's `catalogue_seed.json` entry, rerun step 4 for that book too and check whether the changed fields need updating in `processed/catalogue.json`.
6. Run `build_library_db.py`.

## Source file quirks (`tools/process_book.py`)

- Source EPUBs are Internet Archive OCR exports: one XHTML file per original page (page number recoverable from the `<title>` tag), one `<p>` of OCR'd text per page. Some also embed a full-resolution JPEG scan of every page — `process_book.py` reads only the `<p>` text and never copies these images into `processed/`, since that's what made the raw Charaka Samhita EPUB ~1GB. Do not add image-preservation logic without discussing the size tradeoff first.
- Four sources are raw PDFs handled by a separate `parse_source_pdf()` path using `pypdf`'s per-page text layer, with no EPUB conversion involved: `Samuel Hahnemann - Organon of Medicine.pdf`, `DGHS - The Unani Pharmacopia of Bangladesh - Part 1 Volume 4 (2020).pdf`, `AYUSH - National Formulary of Unani Medicine - Part 2 Volume 1 (2007).pdf`, and `Oushadhi - Therapeutic Index - 5th Impression (2019).pdf`. Page numbers there are raw PDF page indices, not printed folio numbers (though for the three government-formulary PDFs the index happens to line up with each page's own printed footer number). These are distinct from the `raw/general/` books below, whose source PDFs were converted to EPUB *before* being placed in `raw/` rather than kept as PDFs.
- `micozzi-fundamentals-of-complementary-and-alternative-medicine-5th` and `ernst-oxford-handbook-of-complementary-medicine` aren't from Internet Archive at all — the user supplied PDFs (the Micozzi one born-digital with real text/TOC/images; the Ernst one an Internet-Archive-OCR'd scan with a real text layer but no embedded TOC and ~900 JPEG2000 images, which aren't a valid EPUB format and were dropped as likely OCR artifacts) that were each converted to EPUB with the same one-off script (not part of this repo) mimicking the standard `<title>Page N</title>` + one-`<p>`-per-page layout, specifically so they'd pass through the *ordinary* `parse_source_epub()` path unmodified. That conversion script no longer exists anywhere in this repo; if either book ever needs reprocessing from its original PDF, it must be rebuilt (page text + TOC + images) from scratch, since only page text survived into `raw/general/`'s EPUBs and then into `processed/`. One piece of that lost TOC did survive as a side effect, though: the conversion script's `nav.xhtml` for Micozzi still carries the original textbook's real 43-chapter TOC with per-chapter page anchors (Ernst's `nav.xhtml` is just a single placeholder entry, no such luck). `process_book.py`'s generic parser never reads `nav.xhtml`, so this went unused until `detect_chapters_from_epub_nav()` (see below) started reading it directly — no need to rebuild the lost script just for chapters.
- `heinrich-fundamentals-of-pharmacognosy-and-phytotherapy-4th` is a different case again: a genuine Elsevier retail EPUB with real EPUB3 semantic structure (`epub:type="pagebreak"` spans carrying the actual printed page number as `aria-label`, including roman numerals for front matter; `<h1 class="chaptitle">` for real chapter headings) but body text inside `<div>` elements, not `<p>` tags. Critically, the *ordinary* `parse_source_epub()` does **not** cleanly fail on it — it silently extracts ~26 garbage pseudo-pages from incidental `<p>` tags elsewhere in the file (marketing boilerplate, duplicated TOC fragments, all with `page_number: None`), which is enough to pass the "no pages extracted" check and would silently overwrite the correct `processed/` output with garbage. This is why `REQUIRES_DEDICATED_SCRIPT` in `process_book.py` explicitly skips this id in both `--all` and single-id mode with a clear message, rather than letting it fall through to the generic parser. It was actually processed with a second one-off script (also not in this repo) that walks the real pagebreak/chapter markup directly and calls this file's own `build_book_json()`/`build_epub()` — meaning its `page_number` values and `chapters[]` array are the actual book's real pagination/TOC, meaningfully higher fidelity than every other book's guessed/regex-derived structure. If this book ever needs reprocessing, that script must be rebuilt first; don't remove it from `REQUIRES_DEDICATED_SCRIPT` without one ready.
- A few sources (currently two Bengali facsimiles: `idris-ali-bissoykor-lokkhone-homoeopathic-chikitsa`, `pandey-homoeopathic-practice-of-medicine`) have **no text layer at all** — every page is only a scanned image. These are marked `"source_format": "facsimile-epub"` in `catalogue_seed.json` and routed to `parse_source_facsimile_epub()`, which shells out to the `tesseract` CLI per page image using the entry's `"ocr_lang"` (`ben+eng` for both — plain `ben` badly mis-recognizes embedded Latin homeopathic remedy names, e.g. "Glonion 1x" read as garbage digits). This requires `tesseract` and the relevant `.traineddata` installed locally (not in `tools/requirements.txt`, since it's a system binary, not a pip package) — see the README's "Processed versions" section. OCR quality here is a genuine new artifact (errors introduced by this pipeline), unlike every other book's OCR (already present in the source, just cleaned up) — don't conflate the two when reasoning about text quality. New facsimile sources follow the same pattern: move/rename into `raw/`, add a catalogue entry with `source_format`/`ocr_lang` set, run `process_book.py <id>` (budget real time — Tesseract runs at roughly 1-1.5s/page, so a few-hundred-page book takes several minutes; run it in the background rather than blocking on it).
- OCR cleanup is intentionally light-touch: Unicode NFKC normalization and whitespace collapsing only. Garbled OCR (e.g. "CHAR AKA samhitA" for "CHARAKA SAMHITA") is left as-is by design — re-OCRing from page images was explicitly deferred, not overlooked.
- Rebuilt EPUBs preserve pagination via a `<span id="page-N">`/`<p class="pagenum">` anchor at each original page boundary. Chapter/heading-level TOC detection is *not* attempted generically — the source OCR has zero heading markup (no `<h1>`-`<h6>` anywhere) — but `CHAPTER_PATTERNS` in `process_book.py` adds book-specific chapter extraction for two books where a reliable canonical opening phrase exists in the OCR text itself:
  - `charaka-samhita-kaviratna-1892`: `LESSON <roman>. And now we shall expound the Lesson called/on <title>` (and phrasing variants)
  - `sushruta-samhita-vol-2-1911`: `CHAPTER <roman>. Now we shall discourse on <title>`
  These patterns were reverse-engineered from the actual OCR text and deliberately require the full opening phrase, not just the bare "LESSON N"/"CHAPTER N" token, because both books also mention chapter numbers in table-of-contents pages and footnote cross-references that must NOT be treated as chapter starts. Detected chapters land in `book.json`'s `chapters` array and the EPUB's `nav.xhtml`. Coverage is best-effort (OCR noise causes some real lessons/chapters to be missed) — do not treat the list as exhaustive.
  - The **Bengali** Charaka Samhita edition (`charaka-samhita-satish-chandra-sharma-bengali`) was investigated too but deliberately excluded: its chapter markers ("N অধ্যায়") suffer heavy OCR numeral corruption (e.g. ২ misread as ই), making reliable ordinal parsing impractical without a from-scratch Bengali OCR-correction effort. Revisit only if that book's OCR quality improves (e.g. via re-OCR).
  - No other book among the original 15 has any chapter/section structure in its OCR text at all (verified by scanning all 15 for `CHAPTER`/`LESSON`/`PART`/`SECTION`/Bengali equivalents) — most are alphabetical materia medica/repertory references with no narrative chapters to begin with. The two later-added Bengali facsimile books (`idris-ali-bissoykor-lokkhone-homoeopathic-chikitsa`, `pandey-homoeopathic-practice-of-medicine`) were investigated for real structure on 2026-09-25 — see the dedicated bullet below for what was found (one has none, the other has real structure OCR quality can't recover).
  - Two more books get chapters through mechanisms other than `CHAPTER_PATTERNS`, added 2026-09-25 after auditing every book for recoverable structure (not just the original 15): `oushadhi-therapeutic-index-2019`'s raw PDF has its own embedded outline/bookmarks (17 entries, by medicine category), read via `detect_chapters_from_pdf_outline()` using `pypdf`'s `.outline` + `get_destination_page_number()` rather than any text regex — `PDF_OUTLINE_CHAPTER_BOOKS` in `process_book.py` lists which ids use this path. `micozzi-fundamentals-of-complementary-and-alternative-medicine-5th` gets its 43 chapters from its raw EPUB's `nav.xhtml` (see the micozzi bullet above) via `detect_chapters_from_epub_nav()`, listed in `EPUB_NAV_CHAPTER_BOOKS`. Both are higher-fidelity than the `CHAPTER_PATTERNS` regexes — they come from a real embedded outline/TOC, not a reverse-engineered opening-phrase match — so, unlike the charaka/sushruta coverage, these are not "best-effort": every chapter the source's own outline/TOC lists is captured. The same audit also checked every other book's raw source for an embedded PDF outline or a real (non-stub) EPUB `nav.xhtml`/`toc.ncx`, and for OCR-text chapter/part/section keyword hits, turning up several more real-structure candidates not yet wired in at the time (`ernst-oxford-handbook-of-complementary-medicine`, since wired -- see below) and ruling out several false-positive-only ones (`kings-american-dispensatory-vol1/2-1905`, `palmer-science-art-philosophy-of-chiropractic-1910`, `barnes-herbal-medicines-3rd`, `hahnemann-organon-of-medicine`) — see chat history for the per-book evidence if revisiting.
  - `madhava-nidana-vol-1-lochan` (added same day) needed a third mechanism, `detect_chapters_madhava_nidana()` (`STATEFUL_CHAPTER_BOOKS`), because a plain `CHAPTER_PATTERNS`-style stateless regex isn't enough here: this book's OCR chapter numbers aren't reliably legible on their own. Real headings have a distinctive, reliable shape — ALL-CAPS `CHAPTER N` immediately followed by the Devanagari chapter title, its transliteration, and (usually) a parenthetical English gloss, e.g. `CHAPTER 9 दाहनिदानम्‌ Daha Nidanam (...BURNING...)` — which is what excludes both false-positive sources found here (the front-matter TOC, and mixed-case inline citations like "Chapter 24 of ... Text" elsewhere in the prose). But the number itself needs active repair: OCR sometimes renders "1" as a digit-like character (`I`/`]`/`!`), and, critically, sometimes drops a two-digit number's leading "1" outright (13→"3", 17→"7", 18→"8", 19→"9") rather than misreading it — indistinguishable from a real single-digit number without tracking the running sequence. The function reconstructs the intended number by retrying with a "1" prefix whenever a new match wouldn't otherwise exceed the previous chapter's number. Verified against this book's own front-matter TOC titles for every chapter below 33. Coverage stops there: past chapter ~32 the OCR text stops saying "CHAPTER" anywhere in the body at all (checked across the rest of the book), so chapters 33-70 (this book's TOC lists 70; recall from the catalogue note that this volume is Volume I of II and doesn't cover the full canonical 70 anyway) aren't recoverable from this OCR text by any means found so far. Even within the covered range, chapters 9 and 11 are missing for the same reason — no "CHAPTER" heading text at all on those pages. 30 of 32 possible chapters recovered.
  - `adams-practical-guide-to-homeopathic-treatment-1913` (added same day) needed `detect_chapters_adams()` (`ADAMS_CHAPTER_BOOKS`): real headings are ALL-CAPS `PART N.`/`CHAPTER N.` (a lowercase "Chapter" elsewhere is always an inline cross-reference), but the front-matter TOC (pages 1-20) is *also* ALL-CAPS in the same shape and has to be excluded by page number instead. Chapter numbering restarts at "I" within each of the book's 3 Parts, so labels are qualified ("Part II, Chapter I"). Actual yield is small and fully verified: Part I has chapters I-IV, Part II has exactly one chapter (its "Diseases and Their Treatment" reference material isn't subdivided by chapter beyond that), and Part III (Materia Medica) has none at all — confirmed by scanning the *entire* book for every remaining case-sensitive "CHAPTER" occurrence (there are none outside those 5, plus the 2 Part-level headings). 7 entries total, all confirmed real, not best-effort.
  - `bhavaprakasha-vol-1-srikantha-murthy-2001` (added same day) needed `detect_chapters_bhavaprakasha()` (`BHAVAPRAKASHA_CHAPTER_BOOKS`): real headings look like `Chapter-6 (XVI) ... Takra Varga (Group of Butter milks) ...` — chapter 6 alone has 24 roman-numeral sub-chapters (its materia-medica "varga" groups) and chapter 7 has 7 (procedural "prakarana" sections), so this needed the same kind of number-repair logic as Madhava Nidana, but for roman numerals instead of arabic digits. The sub-chapter numeral is trusted whenever it parses as *strict, well-formed* roman numerals (OCR garbles some past recognition on purpose, e.g. "TIT" for "(III)", "VIL" for "(VIII)" — these fail strict validation and fall through to reconstruction) and is only synthesized as (previous sub-number for that chapter) + 1 when missing/unparseable. This distinction mattered in practice: chapter 6's sub-chapter XV has no extractable heading at all, and an earlier draft of this function that used a plain positional counter instead of trusting valid OCR values got every subsequent sub-chapter's roman numeral off by one as a result (mislabeling the real "(XVI) Takra Varga" as "(XV)") — trusting valid OCR digits and only bridging actual gaps avoids that trap. Two false-positive sources excluded: footnote citations in verse-numbering style ("Bh. pr. Chapter 2/1") and appendix cross-references ("Ref. Chapter- 6 ..."). This book's OCR is comparatively heavily garbled, so unlike the Oushadhi/Micozzi/Adams mechanisms, titles here are explicitly best-effort and may retain OCR noise, same caveat as the original CHAPTER_PATTERNS books. 35 chapters/sub-chapters recovered (chapters 1-5 standalone, 6 has 23 of 24 sub-chapters, 7 has all 7).
  - `ernst-oxford-handbook-of-complementary-medicine` (wired in 2026-09-25 via `detect_chapters_ernst()`, `ERNST_CHAPTER_BOOKS`): real "CHAPTER N Title" headers exist (e.g. "CHAPTER 3 Complementary therapies") but repeat as a running page header on *every* page within that chapter (35 occurrences for chapter 3 alone, one per page), not just the opening page. Unlike every other book's mechanism above, the fix here isn't a false-positive filter -- every match is a real, correctly-labeled occurrence, there are just far more of them than there are chapters -- so the function just keeps the first (lowest page number) occurrence per chapter number. All 7 chapters recovered, monotonically increasing with no gaps -- not best-effort.
  - After the above, every remaining chapter-less homeopathy book (not just the "original 15") was swept for recoverable structure, 2026-09-25. One more real one turned up: `majumdar-homoeopathic-chikitsa-prakaran-bengali` needed `detect_chapters_majumdar()` (`MAJUMDAR_CHAPTER_BOOKS`) — real headings are a Bengali *ordinal word* (not a numeral, so none of the digit-OCR-corruption risk that excluded `charaka-samhita-satish-chandra-sharma-bengali`) immediately followed by "অধ্যায়" ("chapter"), e.g. "দ্বিতীয় অধ্যায়। কর্ণিয়ার পীড়া।" (Chapter 2. Corneal disease.). `_MAJUMDAR_ORDINALS` maps all 24 expected ordinal-word spellings, including the specific OCR misreadings actually observed for several of them (e.g. "ষষ্ঠ"/6th losing its leading letter to "ষ্ঠ", "ত্রয়োদশ"/13th misread as "প্রয়োদশ") — verified against the book's own sequential medical topic order (eye → ear → nose → heart → ... → skin), not guessed. Numbers come from each ordinal word's lookup value directly, not position, so the one genuinely missing chapter (19, no extractable heading anywhere in the OCR text) doesn't shift any later chapter's number the way a positional counter would (see the Bhavaprakasha bullet above for why that distinction matters). No front-matter TOC contamination was found here (each of the 23 recovered numbers appears exactly once), unlike Madhava Nidana/Bhavaprakasha, so no page-range exclusion was needed. 23 of 24 chapters recovered.
  - The rest of the swept books stayed correctly chapter-less: `allen-handbook-of-materia-medica-1889`, `boericke-pocket-manual-of-homoeopathic-materia-medica-3rd`, `clarke-dictionary-of-practical-materia-medica-vol1/2/3`, `hering-guiding-symptoms-vol-1-1879`, `kent-repertory-of-homoeopathic-materia-medica-1897`, `dewey-practical-homoeopathic-therapeutics-1900`, `mattei-electro-homoeopathic-medicine-1891`, `nash-leaders-in-homoeopathic-therapeutics-1901`, `farrington-clinical-materia-medica-1908` (their `PART`/`BOOK`/`SECTION` hits are near-entirely false positives — self-references like "this book," dosage language like "one part water," anatomical "part of the body"); `clinical-materia-medica-bengali` and `homoeopathic-chikitsa-darpan-bengali` (their ভাগ/খণ্ড/অধ্যায় hits are ordinary vocabulary or inline cross-references like "as described in the Hernia chapter," not real headings); and `lee-repertory-of-characteristic-symptoms-1889`, which has exactly 2 real "CHAPTER" headers (Mind, Head) out of a clearly much longer body-region structure visible later only as unnumbered running headers (Eyes, Ears, etc. never got OCR'd with a "CHAPTER" prefix) — too sparse relative to the book's real scope to be trustworthy navigation, left unwired. The two Bengali facsimiles (`idris-ali-bissoykor-lokkhone-homoeopathic-chikitsa`, `pandey-homoeopathic-practice-of-medicine`) weren't rechecked in this sweep — their OCR text is pipeline-generated (Tesseract), not source — but were checked separately right after (see below).
  - `idris-ali-bissoykor-lokkhone-homoeopathic-chikitsa` and `pandey-homoeopathic-practice-of-medicine` (checked 2026-09-25, against the already-processed `book.json`'s Tesseract-OCR'd text, since these facsimiles have no source text layer to check directly): different outcomes. `idris-ali` has no chapter structure at all — its single "অধ্যায়" occurrence in the whole book is "গুরুত্বপূর্ণ অধ্যায়" ("important chapter," a descriptive phrase), not a heading. `pandey` genuinely *does* have real chapter structure, using the identical Bengali-ordinal-word convention as `majumdar-homoeopathic-chikitsa-prakaran-bengali` (same era/style of Bengali homeopathy text, organized by disease/body-system) — but this facsimile's OCR is too degraded to recover it: of 18 "অধ্যায়" occurrences across its 792 pages, only 2 have a cleanly recognizable ordinal word ("অষ্টম"/8th at page 365, "উনবিংশ"/19th at page 683); the rest are either inline cross-references ("as discussed in the heart-disease chapter") or the ordinal word itself came through as pure OCR garbage (`gots`, `a`, `see`, `wrt`, `oars`, `rer`) while the adjacent title text OCR'd fine. 2 confirmed anchors for a 792-page book is the same "too sparse to be trustworthy navigation" situation as `lee-repertory-of-characteristic-symptoms-1889` above, so this was left unwired too. Unlike Lee, though, this isn't a text-quality-independent structural gap -- it's specifically this facsimile's OCR quality ceiling (consistent with the facsimile-epub bullet's note that this book's OCR is a pipeline-introduced artifact, not inherited from the source). A regex fix can't help here; recovering it would need re-OCRing at higher fidelity (different Tesseract config/preprocessing, or a better scan) first.
  - The chapter-less ayurveda/unani books were swept the same way on 2026-09-26. Two more real ones turned up: `latif-yunani-hakimi-chikitsha-pranali-1892` needed `detect_chapters_latif()` (`LATIF_CHAPTER_BOOKS`) — the same "<ordinal word> অধ্যায়" convention as majumdar. One new wrinkle here that majumdar didn't need: inline cross-references use the *locative* form "অধ্যায়ে" ("in the chapter") rather than bare "অধ্যায়", so the pattern excludes them with a negative lookahead for the trailing "ে" -- majumdar's pattern doesn't have this lookahead and got away with it (each of its 23 numbers happened to appear exactly once with no colliding locative-form matches), but don't assume that generalizes; check for it explicitly in any new book using this convention. Also excludes page 7 by page number -- a standalone errata page ("ভ্রম সংশোধন") that happens to cite "অষ্টম অধ্যায়" (chapter 8) as a correction reference. 8 of 10 chapters recovered (1st and 8th have no extractable heading anywhere in the OCR text).
  - `sarkar-grihasther-mushtiyog-o-kobirajer-chikitsa-1897` needed `detect_chapters_sarkar()` (`SARKAR_CHAPTER_BOOKS`), a genuinely different case: like Ernst, its real "<ordinal> পরিচ্ছেদ" headings repeat as a running header on every page of the chapter rather than marking just the opening page -- but unlike Ernst, the chapter number itself needs a Bengali-ordinal lookup, and unlike majumdar/latif, this book's OCR mangles its 3rd chapter's ordinal word ("তৃতীয়") into roughly 20 different unrecognizable spellings, making a lookup table impractical for it specifically. Since the book verifiably has only 3 chapters (confirmed by reading to its own final page, a publisher's ad for the not-yet-released Volume 2), any bare "পরিচ্ছেদ" occurrence that isn't chapter 1 or 2's recognized word is inferred to be chapter 3 -- but only once both 1 and 2 have already been found, so nothing earlier in the book can be mistaken for it. The front-matter TOC (pages before 59) needed its own page-based exclusion here, unlike latif, because it uses the same bare-word (non-locative) heading shape the real chapters do. All 3 chapters recovered, but titles are genuinely messy: chapter 2's start page (97) happens to be a transitional page where the chapter boundary falls mid-paragraph, so its extracted "title" is leftover text from the prior topic (general poison antidotes) rather than chapter 2's real subject (midwifery).
  - Investigated but not yet wired in: `ibn-sina-canon-of-medicine-book-1-gruner-1930` (the Canon of Medicine) has real hierarchical structure -- Part → Thesis → Doctrine → Chapter -- with "THESIS N" as a moderately reliable ALL-CAPS marker (6 theses per Part, confirmed across at least 3 Parts), but title extraction is messy (several blank/garbled titles) and at least one likely false positive was found mixed in; needs more verification before implementing, similar effort tier to Bhavaprakasha. Ruled out entirely: `ashtanga-hridaya-vagbhatta-vol-1` (only self-referential "book" mentions), `ibn-qayyim-healing-with-medicine-of-the-prophet-2003` (all "chapter" hits are references to Qur'anic surahs or generic prose, no numbered structure), `ayush-national-formulary-of-unani-medicine-part2-vol1` and `unani-pharmacopia-of-bangladesh-part1-vol4` (both alphabetical drug-monograph references; "PART"/"SECTION" hits are field labels like "Part used" or botanical "cross section" descriptions, and both were already confirmed to have no/minimal embedded PDF outline).

## Important: `raw/` is not 100% mirrored into git

`raw/ayurveda/Charaka Samhita - English Translation by Abinash Chandra Kaviratna - 1892.epub` (~1GB) is **gitignored**, not tracked. It was committed early on, which pushed `.git` past GitHub's 100MB-per-file hard limit and made every push fail; on 2026-09-22 it was stripped out of all git history with `git filter-repo` (rewriting every commit hash) and the working-tree copy was restored afterward, gitignored to prevent recommitting it. The file still exists locally in `raw/ayurveda/` for reprocessing, it's just not versioned. Its clean 5MB derivative (which *is* tracked) lives at `processed/ayurveda/charaka-samhita-kaviratna-1892/`. If this repo is ever re-cloned, that one raw file will be missing and `process_book.py` can't regenerate its `processed/` output without it being placed back manually.
